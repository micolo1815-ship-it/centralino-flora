Red Gum Eucalyptus

Scientific Name: Eucalyptus camaldulensis
Common Name: Red Gum Eucalyptus
Local Name: N/A
Description:

The red gum eucalyptus, also known scientifically as Eucalyptus camaldulensis, is a flowering tree that thrives in various climates, particularly in Australia and regions with similar conditions. Leaves are deciduous, alternate, lanceolate, acuminate, light green and thin. Inflorescences are axillary, simple, umbels are 5 to 11-flowered; peduncle is 1 to 1.5 centimeters, slender and terete. Flower buds are ovoid, 5 to 8 millimeters. Hypanthium is semiglobose, about 3 millimeters; stipes are 3 to 12 millimeters. Flowers are in umbels. Capsule is subglobose, 5 to 6 millimeters in diameter, disk broad, valves 3 to 5, exserted from the hypanthium.

Uses in Filipino Folklore and Other Uses:

While not traditionally tied to folklore, red gum eucalyptus may be used for its scent and appearance in landscaping within urban settings in the Philippines. The leaves are sometimes utilized in folk medicine for respiratory conditions. Eucalyptus oil, derived from various species, is often applied in aromatherapy and health-related practices.

Tree Facts:
  • Can reach heights of 15 to 25 meters, with distinctive smooth bark.
  • Known for its resilience, it is tolerant of drought and poor soil conditions.
  • Often found near rivers and floodplains, where it helps stabilize ecosystems.
  • Uses in Horticulture: Widely planted for shade, erosion control, and as a source of nectar.
  • The leaves have traditional applications in herbal remedies and are believed to possess antiseptic properties.
