Guava

Scientific Name: Psidium guajava
Common Name: Guava
Local Name: Bayabas
Description:

Guava, scientifically known as Psidium guajava, is an evergreen shrub or small tree that is native to the Caribbean, Central America, and South America. It is known for its sweet, aromatic fruit, which varies in color from green to yellow, pink, or red, depending on the cultivar. Guava trees typically grow to about 3 to 10 meters in height and produce white, aromatic flowers. Guava is highly valued for its nutritional and medicinal properties. It is rich in vitamin C, dietary fiber, and antioxidants. The fruit can be eaten raw or cooked and is often used in juices, jams, and desserts. The leaves are also utilized in traditional remedies.

Uses in Filipino Folklore and Other Uses:

In Filipino folklore, the guava fruit is often featured in traditional stories and legends, with significant moral lessons related to sharing and community. Guava trees are also recognized for their health benefits and are used in various folk remedies, such as treating stomach ailments with guava leaf tea.

Tree Facts:
  • Guava is exceptionally high in vitamin C, containing approximately 228.3 mg per 100g, which is significantly more than oranges.
  • Traditionally, various parts of the guava plant are utilized for their medicinal properties, such as treating gastrointestinal issues, inflammation, and infections.
  • Guava is commonly used in cooking, especially in the Philippines, where ripe guava is an ingredient in sinigang (a sour soup).
  • Guava is featured in Philippine folklore as a symbol of abundance and is often associated with seasonal festivals celebrating local agriculture.
  • Guava trees exhibit a wide variability in their cultivated varieties, which are especially prominent in tropical and subtropical regions.
