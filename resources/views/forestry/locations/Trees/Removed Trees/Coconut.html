Coconut

Scientific Name: Cocos nucifera
Common Name: Coconut
Local Name: Boku, Niyog
Description:

The coconut palm, or Cocos nucifera, is a versatile tropical plant recognized for its wide array of uses, both economically and culturally. It is the only living species of the genus Cocos. The term "coconut" can refer to the whole coconut palm, seed or fruit, which botanically is a drupe, not a nut. The genus name Cocos derives from the old Portuguese word coco, meaning "head" or "skull", after the three indentations on the coconut shell that resemble facial features. The specific name nucifera derives from Latin words nux (nut) and fera (bearing), for 'nut-bearing'.nCoconut is one of the most useful plants in the world, providing a multitude of uses, from arrack to food staple, sugar to vinegar, fibers and fodder, thatching and lumber, and virgin coconut oil among many others. In addition, it yield 3 to 4 tons of copra (nut meat) per hectare and over two tons of oil.

Uses in Filipino Folklore and Other Uses:

The coconut plant plays a significant role in Filipino culture, featured in lore as embodying resilience and sustenance. In traditional Filipino folk medicine, coconut milk and oil are often used for skin issues and digestive health. Coconuts are commonly utilized in numerous recipes, and every part of the coconut—husk, shell, meat, and water—finds practical uses in various crafts and culinary practices. Coconuts are also sold widely in markets, contributing to local economies across the Philippines.

Tree Facts:
  • Can grow up to 30 meters tall with pinnate leaves that form a characteristic crown.
  • The coconut contains coconut water, meat, and can be processed into oil, milk, and copra.
  • Integral to many tropical cultures for sustenance, shelter, and even ceremonial uses.
  • Grown in over 90 countries, thriving in sandy coastal areas and drier regions.
  • Seen as a "tree of life" due to its numerous uses in food, cosmetics, construction, and folk medicine.
