Strangler Fig

Scientific Name: Ficus altissima Blume
Common Name: Strangler Fig
Local Name: Balete
Description:

Ficus altissima is a large, evergreen forest tree, growing to 30 m (98 ft), with a spreading crown and often multiple buttressed trunks and characteristic of its subgenus Urostigma. The bark is smooth and grey, with small pale brown pustules. The branches are spreading and the twigs are hairy and often green when young. The leaves are alternate, elliptic to ovate, with entire margins and up to 100 by 40 mm (3.9 by 1.6 in). They are supported on short stalks and have sheathing stipules. The flowers are solitary or in pairs and are in the axils of the leaves. They are concealed inside hollow receptacles which grow into orangish-red, many-seeded. 25 mm (1 in) figs following pollination.

Uses in Filipino Folklore and Other Uses:

In Filipino folklore, it is believed that balete trees are dwelling places for supernatural beings (anito) like diwata, kapre or tikbalang. And in some places, sorcery rituals are known to be performed inside the chambers formed by the tree.

Tree Facts:
  • Ficus altissima is native to southeastern Asia and many islands in the Pacific.
  • It can also grow as a lithophyte in a crevice in a rock or a man made structure. It is sometimes planted as a shade tree but has a wide root system and is unsuitably large for most urban areas.
  • Called “strangler fig” because often starting life as an epiphyte, on trees such as Lagerstroemia or palms, sending down roots to the ground which in time grow stout enough to support the growing tree independently. By this time the host tree has been overwhelmed and killed.
  • One of many trees that hosts lac insects, scale insects in the superfamily Coccoidea, from which the dye lac is obtained
